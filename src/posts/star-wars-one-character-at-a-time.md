I went looking at ASCII and ANSI art and found something that immediately deserved a place in my bookmarks: [STAR WARS ASCIIMATION](https://www.asciimation.co.nz/).

Star Wars scenes. Animated. With text characters.

Go take a look. I’ll wait.

I like projects that make me think about how much can be expressed with very little. A fixed grid of characters sounds restrictive, but then someone uses it to give you recognizable shapes, movement, and a scene you can follow.

You can see the constraint and the ingenuity at the same time. That is a large part of the appeal for me.

Simon Jansen’s [FAQ explains how he builds the animation](https://www.asciimation.co.nz/asciimation/ascii_faq.html): frames of text displayed in sequence, with timing information controlling how long they remain on screen. He started the project in July 1997.

The underlying idea is easy to describe. Actually drawing and arranging those frames is where the patience and craft show up.

I spend enough time thinking about what software is supposed to accomplish for a customer or a business. It’s refreshing to find a project whose appeal needs so little justification. Someone wanted to make this, worked out how, and made something fun to watch.

That is plenty of reason for it to exist.

I’m linking to the project itself so you can see the animation and read the creator’s explanation. A paragraph about text animation is a poor substitute for watching it happen.

Fair warning: this may lead to a much longer detour through ASCII art than you originally intended.
